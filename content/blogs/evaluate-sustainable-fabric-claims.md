## Key takeaways

- Ask suppliers to connect every sustainability claim to a document, standard, or production process.
- Check whether certifications apply to the mill, the material, the transaction, or only the brand story.
- Review performance, care, durability, and availability before choosing a lower-impact material.

## Start with the exact claim

Words like sustainable, eco-friendly, recycled, responsible, and low-impact can mean different things in different supply chains. Before comparing fabrics, write down the exact claim you need to verify.

For example, a recycled polyester fabric claim should make clear whether the recycled input is post-consumer or post-industrial, whether the percentage is measured by weight, and whether chain-of-custody documents are available.

## Review documents and scope

Certifications are useful, but scope matters. A certificate may apply to a spinning mill, dye house, trading company, or finished fabric. Check the certificate holder, validity period, material category, and transaction requirements.

If a supplier cannot explain how the documentation follows the fabric from input to finished material, treat the claim as incomplete until the missing details are resolved.

## Balance impact with performance

A fabric that looks better on paper may still fail if it cannot meet durability, colorfastness, hand feel, shrinkage, care, or compliance requirements. Sustainable sourcing should include both environmental claims and product performance.

The strongest sourcing decisions combine verified claims, reliable production, and a realistic understanding of how the fabric will be used.
